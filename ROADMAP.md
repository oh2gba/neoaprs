# Roadmap

What is planned.

- A receive-only text-monitor profile
- A registered tocall
- Direwolf inside NeoAPRS, as the FM radio type "Sound card": built from the pinned source on Linux, macOS and Windows like Mercury, with a patch so that its KISS and AGW ports listen on this computer only (no firewall rule on any system), its configuration written by NeoAPRS (the sound card and the keying chosen as for the HF radio, 1200 baud, a port the daemon picks), so that a radio with a sound-card interface (Digirig, AIOC, a radio's own USB audio) needs nothing else installed. One sound card is not shared by the FM and HF radios at once.
- 80 m among the suggested HF frequencies: 3.5850 MHz USB proposed (the signal at about 3.5862 to 3.5868 MHz), inside the IARU Region 1 digimode segment, above WSPR, FT8, FT4, JS8 and PSK31 (all below about 3.582) and below the automatic segment from 3.590, which RTTY contests also use at weekends; a listen on the band first. The HF box then holds four lines (`MAX_FREQUENCIES`), and the list goes by band: 80, 40, 20, 6 m.
- The suggested frequencies in the HF box aligned as on the project page: the bands right-aligned and the decimal points one under another, in the mono font.
- The app in the project page's colours, so that the two look the same: the Vaisala palette of `site/style.css` (petrol #0a2a3d, #0e1f2c and #182a34; white, #eff0f2 and #5d5d5d; mint #a6d7d4 and sage #91c1b9; sky #bbdaed, blue #60addb and #2972a3; lime #dbf93a; orange #fb6432 and #e54615) in `ui/src/styles/tokens.css` and `docs/palette.md`, light and dark (the waterfall keeps a rig's colours), and every screenshot of the page taken again after it.
- Installing the page on a phone as an app made easy. The page is a PWA already (the manifest, the service worker, the icons), but a phone installs it only over the secure connection, which is off by default, and with NeoAPRS's certificate installed on the phone, three steps that nobody finds; over plain http a phone gets only a shortcut on the home screen. An Install button where the browser allows it, and one guided way there from the phone itself: the secure connection, the certificate for that phone, the install.
- The desktop window by one toolkit on every system: pywebview on Linux and macOS too (WebKitGTK and WKWebView, the system's own web views), as on Windows, in place of Qt and its web engine, which is a whole Chromium inside the AppImage and the macOS app. A trial first, since the map (MapLibre, WebGL) must draw as well in WebKitGTK as in Chromium; where it does not, Linux opens the browser and keeps the tray icon.
- Any number of radios of any type (two FM, several HF on different frequencies, a LoRa), each with its own name; every control of the page and the setup guide follow the radios that are there.
- Skeds: a board of who will be on the air, when and on which frequency, so that the few stations on HF can agree on a time to hear each other. It is shared through neo.oh2gba.eu and never sent by radio.
- The HF waterfall at the modem's own resolution, with a frequency ruler and the spectrum as a curve; a monitor card on the map; and the signal-to-noise ratio shown on what is heard.
- Updates signed, so that Update now installs only what the project made.
- The Windows installer and the macOS app signed by a publisher's certificate, so that Windows and macOS stop warning when NeoAPRS is installed. It waits until it can be done without cost: the owner pays for nothing, and a free programme for open source would need his application. (The signing of updates, the line above, is another thing and costs nothing: its key is the project's own.)
