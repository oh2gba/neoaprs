NeoAPRS

A modern, easy-to-use APRS client: the stations you hear on a map, messages that get acked, weather, the beacon, fixed or from a GPS. One app, or a service you open from anywhere at home, on a laptop, a tablet or a phone.

The map

Everything you hear, on one map

  • APRS symbols and calls on a street-level map of your area, made once and kept on your disk.
  • Trails that fade with age. A green ring marks a station heard direct; markers dim as a station ages.
  • A thin dashed ALOHA circle round your station: the distance within which the stations you hear would fill the channel, so a packet for a station beyond it is hard to receive.
  • Point at a station for a quick card: status, comment, distance, how it reaches you. Click it for the full card, and its route is drawn on the map, through each digipeater.
  • The card says what the station runs (a Kenwood TH-D74, APRSdroid, an OpenTracker), and under Details its antenna's direction as an arrow and how many packets came in the last hour. A pin keeps a card on the map, past a reload.

The Stations table

  • Sort, filter, hide the timed out, keep the stations heard direct.
  • On map for chooses how long a station stays on the map after its last position, from 1 to 24 hours, for every browser and device at once; the table keeps it, dimmed.
  • Distance and bearing from you (a small arrow), when each station was last heard, and a check mark for the ones heard direct.
  • An envelope to message a station and a trash can to forget it on every row, and a menu (right-click, long press or the ... button) with the APRS queries: ask a station for its position, its status, what it hears direct, or trace the route to it.

Several stations at once

  • Ctrl+click (Cmd+click on a Mac) on a marker or a row of the table adds a station's card beside the others: each one is the whole card, up to four on a desktop.
  • Drag a card by its head to put it where you want it over the map; the others stay where they are.
  • Close all, or Message these 2 to send one text to each of them.

The packet log

  • Every packet as it was heard, and the route it came by.
  • Click one to see what the parser made of it.

What is around you

The local APRS, at a glance

  • The Nearby tab lists the digipeaters, the voice frequencies, the iGates and the stations heard direct, each list nearest first with the distance and bearing; a click flies the map to the station.
  • The digipeater you pick has its range circle on the map, one at a time, and the map is fitted to it.
  • A station that gives its voice frequency (145.775MHz T118 -060) has it on its card as a line: the tone, the offset and the range. Storm data and signposts are decoded too.
  • The APRS queries are answered, ?APRSH included, and objects you keep can wait to be asked for: your own QRU server, as in APRSISCE/32 and YAAC.

Messaging

Messages that arrive, and proof that they did

  • Acks, reply-acks and retries are done for you.
  • The delivery chain of every message: each digipeater you hear repeat it turns green, one you do not hear stays grey with a ?, a copy that came back from the internet is blue.
  • The conversations in a list beside the open one, as in a chat app; one text to several stations at once from their cards.
  • A message chooses its own path: the way a station was lately heard to be reached, then your beacon's path. A path for each station if you want one.
  • Alerts on the desktop and in the browser, bulletins and group messages, and the APRS queries.

Weather

Weather stations, with graphs

  • Temperature, wind and gusts, humidity, pressure and rain, as the station reports them.
  • 24 hour and 7 day graphs of temperature, pressure, wind and rain, drawn in the page's own colours. Each line is one smooth curve across the hours where the station sent nothing, and never rises above or falls below its readings.
  • Open several weather stations side by side: their graphs share the same scales, so the curves can be read against each other.

Beacon and status

Your position, on your terms

  • A beacon status at the top: when the last beacon went out and when the next is due, with Beacon now and Send status now, and the beacon every 30 or 60 minutes or every 3 hours.
  • Your place chosen on the map or typed as coordinates or a locator, your symbol picked from pictures, and a preview of the packet it makes.
  • A recommended path that follows your symbol: WIDE1-1,WIDE2-1 for a station that moves, WIDE2-1 for one that stays.
  • A status report from your radio through Hamlib rigctld, or a text of your own: one choice, and only the fields it needs.
  • A fill-in digipeater for WIDE1-1, one tickbox, off until you switch it on.
  • Settings apply as you change them: no Save button, and an Undo for the last change.
  • Nothing is transmitted until a call is set, and the Beacon choice switches the beacon off whenever you want (below).

A moving station

  • One choice, Position: Fixed, A GPS via gpsd or A GPS on a serial port. A GPS makes the beacon follow the fix of the receiver.
  • SmartBeaconing decides when it goes: often when you move fast or turn a corner, rarely when you stand still, with a preset for By car, By bicycle, On foot and By boat.
  • A compressed position with course, speed and altitude. With no fix nothing is sent, and never your home position.
  • On the map you move with the fix, and Follow me keeps the map on you. The beacon status shows the GPS and what SmartBeaconing does next.

Listening

A day to listen, without announcing yourself

  • One Beacon choice on the Beacon tab: Off or an interval, and for a moving station Off or On (SmartBeaconing).
  • While it is off there is no beacon and no status report, and what belongs to them is greyed out; messages, acks, answers to queries, objects, bulletins and the digipeater go on.
  • A station that must never key the transmitter at all, a receiver or a test bench, has Receive only as a setting of the daemon: nothing is transmitted, and an amber badge beside the title says so on every screen.
  • The messages being sent wait and go on when transmitting is switched on again; nothing is lost, nothing comes as a burst.

Your radio

One line while it works, and a button that finds it

  • The TNC group of the Settings tab is one line: the state of the radio and when a packet last came, with Reconnect. An arrow opens the type of TNC, the port, the speed and the terminal; it is open by itself when the radio is not connected.
  • Find my radio (Linux) tries the ports and the paired Bluetooth devices at the usual speeds and finds a TNC that talks KISS or can be put in KISS. It writes nothing to the air, and the port, speed and type go into the settings with an Undo.
  • A radio switched off and on again is put back in KISS at once, and the keep-alive no longer deafens a busy channel: it asks only after 10 quiet minutes.

Email and bulletins

Email through the APRS gateway, and the message board

  • Email in a tab of its own: send a mail from a box at the bottom, with Check email for the mail the gateway kept for you.
  • Announcements, bulletins and group messages on a board: who sent each, to which slot, how often it was heard.
  • Post your own to the board from a box under it: a bulletin, an announcement or a group bulletin. It goes out a few times early and then at long intervals, and sits on the board marked ours.

Objects and items

Your own objects, on the map and on the air

  • A net control point, a meeting place, an event: an object with a name, a symbol and a comment.
  • Click the map to place it, drag its marker to move it. Yours carry a dashed ring.
  • Sent on the specification's schedule: at once, then after 1, 2, 4 minutes and so on, up to the net cycle time of its path.
  • Nothing is transmitted until a call is set.

Make one, change it, kill it

  • The Objects tab lists them with how often each was sent and when it goes out next.
  • A click on its row selects it on the map and puts it in the form under the list: symbol, position, comment, a path of its own, a QRU group, and course and speed behind a closed Moving heading. Add makes a new one, Save changes the selected one.
  • A kill is sent three times and the object is gone from the map and from the air.

Languages

The page speaks your language

  • English, Deutsch, Español, Français, Italiano, 日本語 and Português (Brasil).
  • It starts in your browser's language; Language at the top of the Settings tab changes it for that browser.
  • The translations wait for native-speaking hams to read them: corrections are welcome.

Updates

A new version, one click away

  • When a newer version is out, a window says what is new and what to do for the way you installed NeoAPRS.
  • On Windows and with the AppImage, Update now downloads the release, checks its checksum, installs it and restarts. Nothing happens until you press it.
  • For a .deb, pip or the install line the window has the steps, each file a button and each command with Copy.

Open it from another device

  • Switch on Allow access from the network in Settings. The page shows an access key and the addresses to open.
  • Type the key once on each laptop or phone. An optional secure connection (https), with a certificate NeoAPRS makes itself, is one tickbox away.
  • The page fits a phone and a tablet as well as a desktop: on a phone one view at a time, the map or the open tab, a station's card as a sheet from the bottom.
  • A message to you alerts in that browser too: a sound, and a pop-up where the browser allows one (Settings, Notifications).

First start

Five steps, then you are on the air

  • Call, position, symbol, radio, map.
  • Until a call is set NeoAPRS only listens.
  • The map of your area is made once, in a few minutes of internet.
  • Once a day it asks whether a newer version is out, sending its version and system and nothing else.
  • A new version shows in the footer with the steps to update your copy, and Update now for the Windows installer and the AppImage; open pages load it by themselves.
  • On a computer or an Android phone the page can be installed as an app of its own, from localhost or an https address.

On Linux a Bluetooth radio paired with the computer is picked from the Port list by its name, with no extra steps; a Bluetooth LE KISS TNC is a port of its own (ble://, with an extra, not yet tried on a radio). Yaesu and Icom radios have no KISS TNC for a computer: use a sound-card interface and Direwolf. The radio guide marks which entries are tried on the air and which follow the radios' manuals and users' reports.

Get it

Download and run

Windows

Run the installer. No administrator rights are needed. SmartScreen may warn about an unsigned installer: More info, Run anyway.

Windows installer

Linux

Make the AppImage executable and run it.

Linux AppImage

Raspberry Pi, Debian, Ubuntu

The .deb for your machine (arm64 for a Pi, amd64 for a PC) brings its own Python.

Raspberry Pi .deb
$ sudo apt install ./neoaprs_*.deb && neoaprs service install --linger

Linux, macOS, Raspberry Pi

Installs uv and NeoAPRS and starts it as a service of your user.

$ curl -LsSf https://github.com/oh2gba/neoaprs/releases/latest/download/install.sh | sh

No radio at hand?

The sample capture that comes with NeoAPRS plays as a TNC on a TCP port, so you can try the page first. How

$ neoaprs replay
