The Yaesu FTX-1 on APRS: one cable, one setting

One USB cable between the FTX-1 and the computer, one setting changed on the radio, and the FTX-1 is an APRS station: no interface box and no audio leads, and the microphone works for voice as before. The first beacon sent this way, on 144.800 MHz, came back from two digipeaters within four seconds.

What the cable brings

The radio's USB socket gives the computer one hub with three things on it.

A sound cardShown as "USB Audio Device": the radio's receive and transmit audio
Two serial portsOn a CP2105 chip. The RTS line of the second one keys the transmitter
One port moreNot needed for APRS

In NeoAPRS

  1. Settings → Radio → Add radio: Sound card, then Add.
  2. Sound card: USB Audio Device.
  3. Keying: RTS on the second of the radio's two serial ports. On Linux it is the one whose name under /dev/serial/by-id ends in if01-port0.

Test, beside Keying, keys the radio for a second, so you see it go to transmit and come back.

On the radio

A new FTX-1 ignores the computer's keying. Hold FUNC, then RADIO SETTING → MODE FM → RPTT SELECT, and set it from OFF to RTS.

Leave MOD SOURCE on AUTO. The radio then takes its audio from whatever keyed it: the computer's sound when NeoAPRS keys it by RTS, the microphone when you press its PTT.

The two levels are on the radio too, under MODE FM, each from 0 to 100 and 50 when new:

USB OUT LEVELWhat the computer hears
USB MOD GAINWhat the radio sends

To key by VOX instead: OPERATION SETTING → TX GNRL → VOX SELECT: USB (MIC when new), and Keying VOX in NeoAPRS. RTS is the more dependable of the two.

On Linux the sound card's own output volume may start very low (it stood at 38 % on the first FTX-1 we tried): raise it with the Transmit level slider in the radio's box, which NeoAPRS then puts back at every start.

The menus are in the radio's Operation Manual, on pages 52 to 54 and 68 to 72.
